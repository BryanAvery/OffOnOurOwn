Our recent journey through the northern parts of the Czech Republic was nothing short of enchanting. While many travellers flock to the vibrant streets of Prague, we decided to venture off the beaten path, discovering the lesser-known but equally captivating regions of this beautiful country. From the luxurious spa town of Karlovy Vary to the historic Plzeň brewery, our trip was filled with delightful surprises and unforgettable experiences.

Karlovy Vary: A Spa Town Steeped in History and Elegance

If you're looking for a destination that exudes charm and sophistication, Karlovy Vary should be at the top of your list. Nestled in a lush valley surrounded by forested hills, this spa town has been a haven of relaxation and rejuvenation for centuries. The town's thermal springs, famous for their healing properties, have attracted visitors from all over Europe, including royalty, artists, and writers. The grand colonnades and pastel-coloured buildings that line the river give Karlovy Vary a fairytale-like quality, making it a place where you can easily lose yourself in its beauty and tranquillity.

But Karlovy Vary is more than just a pretty face. The town is a living museum of Bohemian culture and history. Walking through its streets, you can't help but feel the echoes of a bygone era when the town was at the heart of the Bohemian lifestyle. Bohemia, once a kingdom in the Holy Roman Empire, has a rich and complex history that is intertwined with the Czech Republic's own story. The region was a centre of culture and art, known for its distinctive style that combined traditional craftsmanship with an avant-garde spirit.

During World War II, Karlovy Vary, like much of the Czech Republic, was occupied by Nazi Germany. The town was used as a base for high-ranking officials, and its luxurious hotels were converted into military hospitals. The war left its mark on the town, but in the years following, Karlovy Vary was restored to its former glory, continuing to welcome visitors seeking the restorative powers of its springs.

Plzeň: A Brewer's Paradise

No trip to the Czech Republic would be complete without a visit to Plzeň, the birthplace of one of the world's most famous beers—Pilsner. The Plzeňský Prazdroj brewery, known internationally as Pilsner Urquell, is a must-see for any beer enthusiast. The brewery tour offers a fascinating glimpse into the history of Czech brewing, which dates back to the Middle Ages. You'll learn how the innovative brewing techniques developed in Plzeň revolutionised beer-making and gave birth to the golden lager that is now enjoyed around the globe.

As you walk through the brewery, the smell of malt and hops fills the air, and the sight of the massive copper brewing vats is awe-inspiring. The tour culminates in a tasting session in the brewery’s historic cellars, where you can sample unfiltered and unpasteurised Pilsner Urquell straight from the barrel—a taste experience that is as authentic as it gets.

A Journey Through Czech History

Our travels also provided a deeper understanding of the Czech Republic's tumultuous history. From its days as part of the Bohemian Kingdom to the struggles of World War II, the country has seen its fair share of hardship and resilience. During the war, the Czech lands were occupied by Nazi Germany, and the repercussions were severe. The Czech resistance movement was strong, but the cost was high, with many lives lost in the fight for freedom. Following the war, the country fell under Soviet influence, leading to decades of communist rule before finally gaining independence in 1989 with the Velvet Revolution.

Despite these challenges, the Czech people have preserved their rich cultural heritage, and it is evident in everything from the architecture to the food. Speaking of food, we were pleasantly surprised by how affordable everything was, from dining out to accommodation. The prices were far lower than we expected, especially in comparison to other European destinations. Even the campsites were incredibly cheap, making it an ideal destination for budget-conscious travellers.

The Road Less Travelled

One of the most striking aspects of our journey was the lack of British tourists. Outside of Prague, we hardly saw any British cars on the road or encountered fellow Brits, which made the experience feel even more authentic and unspoiled. It was a refreshing change from the often crowded tourist hotspots and allowed us to connect more deeply with the local culture.

With an exchange rate of £1 to 29.55 CZK, we found that our money went a long way, making it possible to indulge in experiences and meals that would have cost much more in other parts of Europe.

Final Thoughts

Our trip through the northern Czech Republic was a revelation. It’s a region that offers so much more than just the well-trodden paths of Prague. From the serene beauty of Karlovy Vary to the historical richness of Plzeň, and the enduring spirit of the Czech people, every moment was filled with discovery. For those seeking a journey that combines history, culture, and natural beauty, the Czech Republic is a destination that should not be overlooked. Whether you're soaking in the therapeutic waters of Karlovy Vary or raising a glass of Pilsner in Plzeň, you're sure to leave with memories that will last a lifetime.

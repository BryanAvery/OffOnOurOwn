After a week of temples, traffic, and the occasional communist icon, paradise arrived in the form of a boarding pass marked SkyBoss. Our VietJet flight to Phu Quoc turned into an unexpected masterclass in VIP treatment, our own private bus, front-row seats, and luggage that appeared before anyone else’s. If this was socialism, we were entirely on board.

Our new home, a beachside apartment at Anja Resort, came with a bed large enough to host a United Nations summit and a view of the sea so close you could practically brush your teeth in it. One sunset cocktail later, we were horizontal, lulled to sleep by the sound of waves and air conditioning heroics.

Friday: The Art of Doing Absolutely Nothing

Friday was an ode to laziness. We alternated between floating in the pool and bobbing in the sea, which, while bath-warm and beautifully blue from a distance, turned out to contain more floating debris than expected. “It’s exfoliating,” Bryan insisted. “It’s alarming,” countered Dana.

By mid-afternoon we’d achieved that classic tourist shade, pink enough to make a flamingo look underdressed, despite hourly sunscreen applications. We consoled ourselves with icy beers and the soothing knowledge that peeling skin is just nature’s way of giving you a souvenir.

That evening we hopped in a Grab taxi (Vietnam’s answer to Uber, only cheaper and infinitely braver) to the Dương Đông night market. Imagine a neon aquarium crossed with a seafood festival, lobsters waving their claws in protest, crabs bubbling ominously, and Bryan turning increasingly green at the sight of it all. “It’s like dining in an episode of Finding Nemo,” he muttered.

We chose a riverside café, more for the view than the menu, and watched the fishing boats glide into the night. On the stroll back, a sudden tropical downpour sent us darting into a coffee roastery, where the smell of freshly ground beans almost made us forget we were drenched. Moments later, a battalion of massage ladies descended like cheerful vultures. “Full body, very cheap! Aloe for sunburn!” they sang. For £5, it felt rude to refuse.

Dana chose a gentle foot rub. Bryan, heroic as ever, went all in. His back, freshly roasted from the sun, devoured the aloe vera like it was a sponge in a drought. The masseuse cracked his spine, his neck, and possibly his will to live, but by the end he looked positively reborn.

Saturday: Pearls and Misplaced Destinations

Having learned the hard way about the sun’s vengeance, we sought indoor entertainment. Cue the Pearl Museum, a glittering temple of salesmanship where staff explained everything except why oysters actually make pearls. Bryan, ever the amateur scientist, provided his own lecture: “Basically, they get irritated and sulk themselves beautiful.” Dana nodded. “Like you, then.”

Then came the day’s adventure, the world-famous Hòn Thơm cable car. Bryan confidently typed the destination into Grab, which led us thirty minutes down a dirt track to someone’s shed. After recalibration (and a small marital debate about Google Maps’ trustworthiness), we finally arrived at the right station.

Tropical Trials and Tubes of Terror: The Phu Quoc Water Park Fiasco

If paradise came with a health warning, it would read: Beware of Sunburn, Strong Cocktails, and Slides That Laugh at Physics.

After the serenity of Hạ Long Bay and the chaos of Hanoi, Phu Quoc was supposed to be our calm chapter. The plan was simple, cocktails, sunsets, perhaps a little snorkelling. Instead, we found ourselves clinging to inflatable rafts, screaming like contestants on a low-budget survival show.

It all began with the cable car of destiny. At eight kilometres long, it’s the Guinness World Record holder for “Most Likely to Terrify Tourists Who Thought This Was a Short Ride.” Dana went pale the moment we lifted off. “This is very high,” she murmured, gripping the bar like it owed her money. Bryan, ever the optimist, was already narrating a fake travel vlog. “Here we are, suspended somewhere between heaven and a mild panic attack.”

Fifteen minutes later, we touched down on Hòn Thơm Island,  home to Aquatopia Water Park, also known as “Where Dignity Goes to Die in a Bikini.”

Dana took one look at the towering slides and muttered, “We can just look, right?” Bryan, eyes gleaming like a child in a sweet shop, replied, “Only if ‘look’ means ‘plummet at 50 miles an hour.’”

The Lazy River: A False Sense of Security

We started gently. The Lazy River wound its way through pirate caves and fake tropical forests. We floated serenely, sipping imaginary piña coladas, both thinking, this isn’t so bad.

Python Plunge: Gravity’s Revenge

“Looks harmless,” Bryan said confidently, staring up at what was basically a bright yellow tube of doom. Dana squinted at the near-vertical drop and muttered, “In what universe does that look harmless?”

We climbed the steps together, clutching a double rubber ring, one of those cheerful-looking inflatable doughnuts that lull you into a false sense of security before trying to end your bloodline. The lifeguard gave us a grin that said I’ve seen things, pushed us forward, and suddenly… nothing. The world just dropped away.

For two horrifying, hilarious seconds, we were airborne. Then we were plummeting, Dana in the front, Bryan behind, both screaming in perfect stereo. “We’re flying!” Dana shrieked. “We’re dying!” Bryan corrected.

The slide twisted, plunged, and spat us out like toothpaste from a tube, hurling us across the splash pool in a glorious explosion of water and British-Czech shrieking.

When we finally surfaced, clinging to the ring like shipwreck survivors, Dana gasped, “I think I swallowed half of Vietnam!” Bryan nodded solemnly. “That was the most romantic near-death experience we’ve ever had.”

Medusa’s Trap: International Relations Gone Wrong

Then came Medusa’s Trap, a four-person raft of pure chaos. The queue was not too long, the raft was huge, and we’d just met a lone woman pointing and smiling at us.

“Almaty!” she said brightly. “Kazakhstan!”

Dana’s eyes lit up. “Вы говорите по-русски?”

And just like that, our trio was formed, the United Nations of Panic.

We piled into the raft. “Hold on tight,” the lifeguard said. “You’ll scream.” He wasn’t wrong.

Down we went, plunging into total darkness, spinning so fast Bryan shouted, “We’re being flushed!” The raft rocketed into a giant funnel, spiralling like a toilet at closing time. Our Kazakh friend screamed, Dana screamed, Bryan screamed in British politeness (“OH GOOD LORD!”), and we all burst out laughing before we’d even stopped spinning.

As we stumbled out, the Kazakh woman grinned and pointed back up. “Again?”

Dana, now bonded by trauma, nodded. “Again.”

So we did. Round two. We screamed louder. We spun faster. Somewhere between gravity and hysteria, Dana shouted to Bryan, “You see? Making friends is easy — just risk your life together!”

Anaconda: The Spin Cycle of Doom

By this point, Bryan’s legs were jelly and Dana’s hair had evolved into its own ecosystem. Still, we couldn’t leave without trying Anaconda — the park’s crown jewel of poor decisions.

It began innocently, then plunged us into spirals so tight Bryan thought we’d been sucked into a Dyson. Dana emerged seconds later, dizzy but triumphant. “I think my organs swapped places,” she announced proudly.

The Wave Beach: Because We Clearly Hadn’t Suffered Enough

To recover, we flopped into the wave pool. Peaceful at first, until a rogue wave body-slammed Bryan, knocking his sunglasses off and his dignity out. Dana, gasping with laughter, was immediately taken out by the next one.

We surfaced like two shipwreck survivors, grinning. “This counts as cardio,” Bryan declared. “This counts as trauma,” Dana corrected.

By the time we staggered back onto the cable car, our skin was wrinkled, our voices hoarse, and our new Kazakh friend was waving goodbye like a war comrade.

Cocktails, Fire Shows and Rain Clouds

That evening, we ventured next door to watch the resort’s famous fire show — cancelled, of course, due to rain. So instead, we toasted our aquatic survival with cocktails and bowls of Bún Chả, Vietnam’s smoky, tangy hug in a bowl.

The rain pattered on the thatched roof, the waves sighed in the dark, and somewhere in the distance, another Grab driver was probably rescuing more tourists from a dirt road.

Phu Quoc, with its burnt shoulders, pearls, and rollercoaster water slides, had already claimed us. Paradise, it turned out, wasn’t perfect — but it was perfectly unforgettable.

Next time: island tours, underwater turtles, and whether Bryan’s sunburn can qualify as a new skin tone.

There’s a certain chaos that comes with the night before a big adventure — especially when that adventure involves crossing half the planet. Dana had spent the day pinging between work stress and the realisation that she was about to travel thousands of miles from home. Her Czech practicality was warring with her nerves; Bryan, meanwhile, was quietly revelling in the logistics being just so.

That evening, their house transformed into a little command centre of love and dog hair. Becki and Luke arrived with baby Felix, Leo, and enough snacks to see them through a small apocalypse. They’d moved in to take care of Poppy, Lola, and Douglas — the three furry reasons Bryan and Dana could never be spontaneous without a spreadsheet. The dogs eyed Becki with suspicion at first, then quickly decided she was acceptable once treats were produced.

Becki, now chief chauffeur, drove the two intrepid travellers to Heathrow. Felix babbled in the back, blissfully unaware he was about to have a week of canine chaos at home. Somewhere between the M4 and the motorway lights, they stopped for that most British of pre-holiday rituals — a service station KFC. “It’s not quite Vietnamese street food,” Bryan joked, peeling open a packet of moist towelettes, “but it’ll do for starters.”

Becki, who had already been to Vietnam, told them how it had been her favourite country — “apart from the bit where I saw a dog on a spit.” Dana’s eyes widened. Bryan quickly changed the subject to the weather forecast.

The Hilton at Heathrow turned out to be the perfect calm before the storm: plush carpets, hushed corridors, and an unexpected free breakfast (a small but glorious win in the grand tradition of British travel victories). They dumped their bags, took the lift to the 14th floor, and emerged into the Sky Lounge — a sleek bar with glass walls overlooking the glowing runways.

Dana’s anxiety began to melt away with her Hugo Spritz, while Bryan’s Luna Moon arrived theatrically, complete with a smoke bomb. “It’s like a science experiment,” he said approvingly. “Except this one might actually end well.”

Planes took off beneath them, streaks of light carving into the night, and for the first time all day, Dana smiled — properly smiled. The adventure ahead still felt unreal, but the excitement was beginning to take over.

Later, they sank into a super-king bed with crisp sheets and the comforting hum of Heathrow in the distance. Not even the thought of a 12-hour flight could dampen the moment. Tomorrow, they’d be on their way to Hanoi — a city of horns, heat, and endless bowls of pho.

Tonight, though, was for stillness — and a little sparkle before the spice.

Up, Up and Away (with Champagne and Lost Passports)

The first morning of Dana & Bryan’s Big Vietnam Adventure began not with chaos, but with something approaching serenity — a rare feat for airport mornings. At 6:30 a.m., both of us actually woke up feeling human. The Hilton breakfast was an event in itself: eggs, coffee, and a floor-to-ceiling view of Heathrow’s runway ballet. We sat mesmerised as a Qantas flight from Sydney rolled in, unloading sun-kissed Australians into the greyer half of the world, while others took off to somewhere warm and mysterious — much like we were about to.

Bags dropped, boarding passes in hand, and then came our first act of heroism: Dana spotted a passport abandoned by a fellow traveller. One mad dash later, a very relieved woman was reunited with her ticket to the world. Karma, we thought, was clearly on our side.

Less divine, however, were the self-service bag tags — those sticky, twisty, origami nightmares supposedly designed for “ease of use”. Bryan wrestled one like it was a Rubik’s cube, until a cheerful airport volunteer named Steven came to our rescue. There’s always a Steven at Heathrow.

At security, new rules meant we could take two litres of liquids through. Bryan, never one to waste good hydration, decided to down an entire bottle of water before walking through. “They said it’s just a body scanner,” he muttered. “But what if it really can see everything?” Cue Dana rolling her eyes and muttering, “I married a child.”

A quick detour via duty free — a bottle of Veuve Clicquot (Bryan’s attempt at romance) and some posh shortbread for our soon-to-be hotel hosts — and we were gate-bound. B42, which Bryan instantly associated with the Douglas B-42 Mixmaster bomber. “Perfect,” he said, “we’re flying into adventure aboard a warplane.”

Then came the tannoy: “Would passengers Avery and Smith please come to the desk?” Hearts skipped. Could it be the mythical upgrade? Alas, no. We were simply deemed strong and sensible enough to sit by the emergency exits — “fit and able to open the doors,” the attendant said sweetly. Bryan asked if she could upgrade us upstairs, just in case she’d missed how heroic we were about the passport thing. “Full flight,” she smiled.

Still, the extra legroom was glorious — a stretch-your-toes-and-pretend-you’re-rich kind of luxury. Twelve hours, three films, two meals, and several aisle laps later, we were somewhere over the Bay of Bengal. Dana snoozed serenely; Bryan marvelled at how the world below had become a soft blue quilt.

Next stop: Vietnam — land of lanterns, scooters, and (fingers crossed) fewer baggage tags.

*Saturday 24 October 2026*

There are many sensible ways to spend a Saturday afternoon in Blackpool. You could enjoy a cream tea, admire the Tower Ballroom or shelter from the rain with a coffee.

We decided to pay good money to be threatened with execution.

There were four of us: Dana, Luke, Ellen and me. Blackpool had made it perfectly clear that October was not a month for sunbathing, so an indoor attraction seemed a splendid idea. What we hadn't fully appreciated was that the Blackpool Tower Dungeon specialises in reducing otherwise respectable adults to shrieking wrecks.

## Breakfast before the bloodshed

Our morning had started in considerably more civilised surroundings. A round wooden table was laid for four, with woven placemats, green napkins, little jars of preserves and cups waiting patiently for tea. Outside the bay windows, the sky was a pale, unenthusiastic grey.

<figure class="dg-left"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5915.jpeg" alt="Breakfast table laid for four" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Our last civilised meal before the authorities caught up with us.</figcaption></figure>

We stopped beneath Blackpool Tower for a photograph of Luke and Ellen. The Tower disappeared upwards into a blanket of cloud, while they stood smiling as though nothing alarming was about to happen.

<figure class="dg-right"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5916.jpeg" alt="Luke and Ellen beneath Blackpool Tower" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Luke and Ellen, still blissfully unaware of the charges about to be brought against them.</figcaption></figure>

## Drop dead gorgeous

The first warning was a red neon sign proclaiming **Drop Dead Gorgeous!** It hung above a nest of twisted roots, skulls and pumpkins. A cheerful little corner, provided your idea of interior decoration involves human remains.

Dana and Ellen posed beneath it, grinning happily. The skeleton behind them appeared less enthusiastic, although admittedly it had probably been waiting quite a while.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5917.jpeg" alt="Dana and Ellen beneath the Drop Dead Gorgeous sign" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Drop dead gorgeous. The ladies looked lovely. The chap behind them had seen better centuries.</figcaption></figure>

Nearby, another sign instructed us to **WAIT HERE. Your time will come.** Most visitor attractions would say 'Please queue here'. The Dungeon preferred to make it sound like a medical prognosis.

<figure class="dg-left"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5918.jpeg" alt="Dungeon sign reading Wait Here Your time will come" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Customer service, medieval style. No estimated waiting time, just a threat.</figcaption></figure>

## Guilty as charged

In the courtroom, justice was swift, theatrical and not particularly interested in facts. A furious judge in a white wig brandished his gavel, while a sign announced that the accused stood guilty.

Luke and Dana immediately pointed at Ellen. I joined in. Ellen was left holding a placard reading **DIRTY PEASANT**.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5933.jpeg" alt="The four friends accused in the Dungeon courtroom" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Three witnesses, no evidence and one very dirty peasant. Ellen never stood a chance.</figcaption></figure>

I couldn't tell you what offence Ellen had committed. Apparently, neither could the prosecution. But three people pointing confidently at you is difficult to argue with, particularly when the judge already has his gavel raised.

## The axe factor

The torture chamber was full of chains, cages, ominous tools and enough rusty metalwork to keep a tetanus clinic in business for years.

Naturally, someone handed us axes.

Luke and I looked ready to offer completely unqualified advice on medieval justice. Ellen appeared to have acquired a weapon of her own, while Dana faced Luke with an expression suggesting she had finally found a practical solution to any future disagreement.

<figure class="dg-right"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5936.jpeg" alt="The group posing with medieval axes in the Dungeon" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Four responsible adults, two enormous axes and absolutely no risk assessment.</figcaption></figure>

## Spirits and other unreliable witnesses

By now, the rooms had grown darker and the atmosphere more sinister. Around a Ouija board, we displayed four quite different approaches to the supernatural: disbelief, panic, astonishment and an urgent desire to be somewhere else.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5937.jpeg" alt="The group reacting to a spooky Ouija board scene" loading="lazy"><figcaption>We asked the spirits whether we'd survive. Their silence was not reassuring.</figcaption></figure>

A second photograph placed us in a grim room with a suspiciously motionless body on a table. Our expressions suggested the patient was beyond the help of even the NHS.

<figure class="dg-left"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5931.jpeg" alt="The group in a grisly Dungeon medical scene" loading="lazy"><figcaption>The patient requested a second opinion. So did the rest of us.</figcaption></figure>

Then came a shadowy street, an enormous moon and a hooded figure carrying a scythe. Blackpool Tower loomed in the distance. I like to think I looked courageous. The photograph offers an alternative interpretation.

<figure class="dg-right"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5932.jpeg" alt="The group posing beside the Grim Reaper" loading="lazy"><figcaption>We'd finally met someone having a worse Saturday than us.</figcaption></figure>

## The final drop

And then came the drop.

The photograph captures Luke and Dana gripping the safety bar as the seats plunge. Luke's mouth is open in an expression that could be either terror or an attempt to swallow the entire atmosphere. Dana has her eyes screwed shut, teeth clenched and hair heading skywards, apparently keen to escape independently.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5934.jpeg" alt="Luke and Dana screaming on the Dungeon drop ride" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Luke discovered a new vocal range. Dana's hair attempted to leave the building.</figcaption></figure>

The wonderful thing about the Dungeon is that you know it's all theatre. The ghosts aren't real, the judge has no legal authority and nobody is genuinely going to be chopped up. Yet the moment something lunges from a dark corner, centuries of human evolution take over and your sensible brain goes off for a tea break.

Outside, Blackpool was still damp and grey. The Tower stood reassuringly upright. We had survived the plague, the torture chamber, the supernatural and the prospect of execution, and somehow we were all still speaking to one another.

There is something rather lovely about four adults laughing so much that they forget to be sensible. That was the real souvenir of the afternoon, although the photographs came a close second.

Ellen, unfortunately, remained a convicted dirty peasant.

It is much smarter than any other station we have been to: very clean and more like Paddington! Our train was also very smart. Every half an hour or so, someone came round with food, drinks, newspapers and more. It was all included in our ticket price. There was so much food that we really couldn’t eat it all. We were sharing our compartment this time with a couple of Africans and some Indians. It made a huge difference to the atmosphere having other foreigners in the same compartment. The Indians didn’t want to interact with us at all and wouldn’t even make eye contact. Such a shame. The journey was uneventful and we actually slept quite well. We were due to arrive in Delhi at 8.30 am, but for reasons unknown to us, we finally arrived three hours late! We had already agreed that for our last night in India, we wanted a fairly nice room as we had a lot of packing to do. Bryan said he knew of one, so off we set. Unfortunately, where he took us was on the equivalent of the North Circular in London! So Jody set off in search of a better location, leaving Bryan to rest in the reception area. When she returned, the main road was closed and a parade seemed to be starting up. We didn’t want to miss out, so we stopped to have a quick look before setting off for the other hotel back on Main Bazaar, where all the action and atmosphere is. When we arrived, the man behind reception told us that the parade we had seen earlier would pass right under our bedroom window. Perfect! It was a Sikh festival, with men on horses, bands and Indian-style carnival floats. As we watched from our window, people waved up at us, wanting their photos taken. Petals were being thrown; it was a really colourful sight. All the vehicles were draped in flowers, and people below were giving out flower garlands. Bryan, never one to miss out, nipped down to the street and bought us some rose garlands. They smelled wonderful—really rosy, unlike the roses you buy in England! After a bit of a snooze, we went out for a pot of “special tea” at our favourite Delhi balcony bar. The parade was still going on, and there were lots of stalls giving out free food. Keeping an eye on the time, we decided to finish our pot of tea and go and try some. We were too late: the food had all gone. Hungry by now, we walked around the area a bit longer and ended up buying a bag of peas from a man at a fresh vegetable stall. We had decided to buy some cooking pots and a chapati pan while we were here (we did have all that extra baggage allowance now, after all!), so we went off in search of a kitchen shop. There was so much to choose from that we bought everything we thought we could possibly need, including thali plates, water cups, rice dishes and our chapati pan. The whole lot cost us less than £10. Bargain! Mission accomplished, we went back to our room and dropped off the heavy pans (I’m sure they will be 23 kg all on their own!). We scoffed our bag of peas before heading off to find some proper food. We ate in a grungy basement restaurant where we had been before and knew the food was really good. We shared a table with an artist from Washington, DC. While we were there, we saw elephants walking down the road—in the middle of Delhi! Amazing. Only in Delhi do you see such bizarre things in the middle of a city. We love it! ## Our last day in India For our last day, Bryan went out early for a haircut and head massage while Jody stayed in the hotel and packed the cases. Later, we went out and Jody finally decided to buy a saree. There was some spare fabric in the box, so they offered to make the little top with it. With it being about 1 pm on our last day, that was pushing it a bit. But, in true Indian fashion, the man came and measured her and promised it would be back by 8 pm. Bryan was hungry, so he had lunch in a grotty cafe (he’d pay for that later). We spent the rest of the afternoon looking for an “Ambassador taxi”. These are cars that look really old-fashioned. Maybe they are, maybe they’re not—you really can’t tell in India. They probably have a factory making them brand new somewhere and have had the panel press for 50 years! Jody had her hands hennaed again, and we bought some henna to take back and use on our friends. Then we went to check on the saree. Yep, all made and very pretty, and all for less than a tenner. Bargain! So, for our last night in India, it had to be India Gate. Off we went in our last tuk-tuk and arrived as it was all lit up. We started making a video to record our last thoughts and feelings as we prepared to leave, but, again in true Indian fashion, we were hounded by people wanting photos of us. In good spirits, we obliged for the last time. Back in Main Bazaar for a final wander through the streets, then up to the hotel roof for hot chocolate before bed. **Night night, India…** ## Shelton Hotel - **Room:** 9/10 — beautiful, clean, modern and comfortable. - **Location:** 10/10 — right on Main Bazaar; perfect. - **Staff:** 7/10 — nice. - **Value for money:** 9/10 — the room was excellent for what we paid. - **Other comments:** Brilliant. The festival passed right under our window! - **Would we stay again?** Yes. **Overall area experience: 9/10.** Delhi has been at the heart of our stay in India. The streets here are colourful, loud, crazy, delightful, disgusting, perfumed, smelly, yet wonderful all at the same time. We wouldn’t have missed it for the world…

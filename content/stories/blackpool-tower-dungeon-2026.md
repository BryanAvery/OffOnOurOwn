*Saturday 24 October 2026*

There are perfectly sensible ways to spend a rainy Saturday in Blackpool. You could take afternoon tea, admire the Tower Ballroom or sit somewhere warm with a coffee.

We decided to pay good money to be accused of witchcraft and dropped into a hole.

There were four of us: Dana, Luke, Ellen and me. Outside, Blackpool Tower disappeared into low grey cloud, while Luke and Ellen posed beneath it with the relaxed expressions of people who hadn't yet met the staff downstairs.

<figure class="dg-right"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5916.jpeg" alt="Luke and Ellen beneath Blackpool Tower" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Luke and Ellen, shortly before the justice system took a personal interest in us.</figcaption></figure>

A glowing **DROP DEAD gorgeous!** sign surrounded by skulls and twisted branches gave us a taste of the establishment's hospitality. Dana and Ellen smiled beneath it. The skulls seemed less cheerful.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5917.jpeg" alt="Dana and Ellen beneath the Drop Dead Gorgeous sign" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Drop dead gorgeous. The ladies looked splendid. The skeleton had let itself go.</figcaption></figure>

A second sign declared **WAIT HERE. Your time will come.** Most attractions offer an estimated queue time. Here they offered a prophecy.

<figure class="dg-left"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5918.jpeg" alt="Wait Here Your time will come sign" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Customer service with a distinctly medieval bedside manner.</figcaption></figure>

## The Lift Jester: Going down

Our first encounter was with a mischievous jester who accompanied us into a medieval lift. In ordinary life, a lift is a practical means of changing floors. In the Dungeon, it is an opportunity for someone in questionable headwear to explain that your immediate future looks bleak.

I glanced at Dana.

'Well, at least we're going down,' I offered.

She gave me the look reserved for statements that are technically correct but entirely unhelpful.

## The Dark Chapel: An unholy welcome

We emerged into the Dark Chapel, where a sinister monk introduced the history of Lytham Priory. The shadows, stonework and solemn voice made it clear that this wasn't going to be one of those cheerful heritage tours where someone tells you how monks brewed beer.

I rather liked the atmosphere. Dana was less convinced by my enthusiasm for dark rooms occupied by threatening strangers.

## The Plague Doctor: A very poor bedside manner

Next came Lancashire in 1349, with plague, rats and a doctor whose manner suggested that medical training had yet to catch up with the concept of patient reassurance.

There is nothing quite like being told about disease while standing in a room where everything looks as though it needs disinfecting.

The doctor had the confidence of a man who had never been challenged by a medical regulator. His treatment options did not inspire confidence.

'Would you let him examine you?' I whispered.

'I'd rather take my chances with the rats,' came the reply.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5931.jpeg" alt="The group posing in a grim medical scene" loading="lazy"><figcaption>The patient requested a second opinion. Preferably from someone born after 1349.</figcaption></figure>

## The Torturer: A hands-on demonstration

At Lancaster Castle's torture chamber, the instruments looked capable of persuading almost anyone to confess to anything. Audience participation was apparently encouraged, which is never a phrase you want to hear from someone standing beside medieval restraints.

I tried to appear calm and interested, as though inspecting an unusually complicated piece of garden equipment. The chains, hooks and metalwork suggested that this was not going to end with a complimentary cup of tea.

## The Conspiracy: Trust absolutely nobody

The Conspiracy tested loyalties and secrets. This was particularly unfair, given that the four of us had arrived as friends and were now being encouraged to consider whether friendship was really worth the trouble.

I have always believed myself a dependable travelling companion. The others may have formed different opinions by this point.

## Courtroom: The dirty peasant affair

Judge Blackheart's courtroom was the natural conclusion to all this suspicion. Here, everyone was guilty of something, and the only remaining question was who would be blamed first.

The photograph provides damning evidence. Luke and Dana point straight at Ellen. I join in. Ellen stands holding a sign reading **DIRTY PEASANT**, which is not a title she had requested.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5933.jpeg" alt="The group before Judge Blackheart" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Three witnesses, no evidence and one very dirty peasant. Ellen never stood a chance.</figcaption></figure>

The judge wore an enormous wig and looked furious. I couldn't tell you what Ellen had done. Neither, I suspect, could the prosecution. But three people pointing at you is a formidable case under medieval law.

## The Labyrinth: Google Maps unavailable

The Labyrinth was a maze of mirrors and lost souls. For someone who has confidently followed Google Maps into the wrong place on more than one occasion, it was almost reassuring to find a location where everybody was equally lost.

Every apparent exit produced another reflection. Each turn offered the possibility of freedom, followed immediately by the discovery that we'd been admiring ourselves in a mirror.

'Are you sure it's this way?' Dana asked.

'Absolutely.'

'You've said that before.'

At least Google Maps couldn't make matters worse.

## The Séance: Madame Mary Wade has company

Madame Mary Wade attempted to contact the dead and draw us into the story of Kitty Breaks. The room was dark, the atmosphere tense and the prospect of receiving a message from beyond the grave was distinctly less appealing than it had sounded on the way in.

Around the Ouija board, our faces told four separate stories. One of us looked sceptical, another alarmed, and at least one appeared ready to negotiate directly with the spirits for an early release.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5937.jpeg" alt="The group reacting around a Ouija board" loading="lazy"><figcaption>We asked the spirits if we'd get out alive. They declined to commit.</figcaption></figure>

## The Witch's Curse: Elizabeth Device awaits

At Lancaster Castle, Elizabeth Device awaited her fate. By now, we'd discovered that seventeenth-century Lancashire was not the ideal destination for anyone hoping for a fair hearing.

The accusations, fear and dark surroundings gave the story a sharper edge. Beneath the theatrical scares was the uncomfortable thought that real people had suffered because suspicion had been allowed to become certainty.

For a moment, the jokes could wait.

## The Pendle Witches: Into the woods

The haunted woods of Pendle brought us back into the shadows, with witchcraft accusations seemingly waiting behind every tree. The sound effects were expertly timed to make perfectly rational adults jump at things they knew were coming.

A photograph with axes captured the general mood of our descent into medieval disorder. We looked less like tourists and more like a committee that had taken a very unfortunate turn.

<figure class="dg-right"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5936.jpeg" alt="The four friends posing with medieval axes" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Four responsible adults, dangerous-looking props and not a risk assessment in sight.</figcaption></figure>

Another photograph put us beside a hooded figure with a scythe beneath a huge moon. It was difficult to tell whether we'd met Death or simply another member of staff having a difficult shift.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5932.jpeg" alt="The group beside the Grim Reaper" loading="lazy"><figcaption>We finally found someone having a worse Saturday than us.</figcaption></figure>

## Drop Dead: The final punishment

After surviving the rest of the Dungeon, we reached the Drop Dead ride. A sudden vertical plunge was apparently considered a suitable reward for our cooperation.

The photograph caught Luke and Dana at precisely the moment dignity abandoned them. Luke's mouth was wide open. Dana's eyes were screwed shut, her teeth clenched and her hair apparently trying to escape independently.

<figure class="dg-wide"><img src="/images/Blackpool/2026-10-24/IMG_5934.jpeg" alt="Luke and Dana reacting on the drop ride" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Luke discovered a new vocal range. Dana's hair applied for separate evacuation.</figcaption></figure>

I would like to report that we handled the experience with composure. Fortunately, the photographs make such a claim impossible.

## The Red Lion: Back among the living

At last came the Red Lion, a tavern where survivors could recover with a drink. After the plague, torture, witchcraft and sudden falling, the sight of a bar felt like a welcome return to civilisation.

Outside, Blackpool remained wet and grey, and the Tower still stood reassuringly upright. But we'd spent the afternoon laughing at each other's reactions, finding courage in the least convincing places and enjoying the sort of shared silliness that becomes funnier every time someone produces the photographs.

We had survived twelve stops in the Dungeon and emerged as four free citizens.

Well, three free citizens.

Ellen was still a dirty peasant.

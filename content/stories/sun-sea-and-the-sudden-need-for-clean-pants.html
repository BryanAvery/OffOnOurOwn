Some days on holiday are made for adventure. Others are made for doing absolutely nothing, and we embraced the latter with Olympic-level dedication.

Morning arrived softly at our seaside apartment, curtains breathing in the warm breeze, the lazy hum of waves promising an unhurried day. Breakfast was minimal effort — coffee, toast, and the mutual satisfaction of having zero plans. Dana settled onto the sand with The Let Them Theory by Mel Robbins, occasionally muttering “yes, exactly” under her breath like she was having a one-woman TED Talk. I, meanwhile, was halfway through my Swimmingly audiobook, nodding along to tales of open-water triumph while heroically not moving an inch under a palm tree.

The sea looked calm but carried its usual bitty clarity, the kind that makes you squint at your feet and hope those shadows are not something sentient. Still, it was gloriously warm - like stepping into a giant mug of green tea. We floated for ages, the world reduced to salt, sunshine, and the occasional squeal from Dana whenever a fish brushed past.

When the heat finally nudged us back to shore, salvation came in the form of two coconuts, cracked open and crowned with straws. Dana declared hers “liquid perfection,” while mine came with the faint taste of adventure - and possibly a hint of rum.

The afternoon’s great quest was to the laundrette, which turned out to be far more entertaining than expected. Among the rows of clattering machines and neatly folded shirts sat a tabby cat wearing a small paper hat that read Manager. The owner, a cheerful woman with folding skills that could qualify as performance art, didn’t even blink when a man strolled in carrying an inflatable flamingo and asked if it could go through the spin cycle. She nodded solemnly, as if this was standard practice. Somewhere between the hum of dryers and the faint smell of detergent, I became convinced the cat actually ran the place - probably on commission.

Clothes washed, dried, and smelling faintly less like adventure, we returned to the beach to continue our exhausting schedule of reading, lounging, and doing precisely nothing.

As dusk fell, we wandered next door to the hotel for the evening’s entertainment — a fire show. Barefoot performers twirled blazing batons that carved ribbons of orange into the sky, their reflections dancing across the water. Dana watched wide-eyed, muttering that Czech health and safety would have a collective breakdown.

We ambled home beneath a sky scattered with stars, the air thick with sea salt, coconut, and that fresh-laundry scent of victory. It was, by all measures, a perfect day — no deadlines, no decisions, just sunshine, two coconuts, one philosophical cat, and the comforting knowledge that our pants were finally clean.

Thanks for reading — and if you spotted our Easter egg, we hope it made you smile.

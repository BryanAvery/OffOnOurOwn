Khem Beach, Phu Quoc, paradise in widescreen. The sand was so white it looked photoshopped, the water clear enough to make even a Brit consider snorkelling without complaint. Dana and Bryan arrived armed with books, sunscreen and the smug satisfaction of hiring two sun loungers at the Premier Residences, a luxury resort that looked like it had been designed by someone who truly understood Instagram. At £100 a night, it was the kind of place that whispered “treat yourself” while secretly judging your flip-flops.

For the first hour, everything was bliss. Waves sighed softly, staff glided by with cold towels and coconuts, and the only decision required was whether to turn the page or turn over. Then came her, the Englishwoman who seemed determined to bring the Daily Mail comments section to the beach.

First, she scolded her teenage daughter loudly enough to make the nearby palm trees wince. “You’ll never get any friends with that attitude!” she bellowed. Then, having established her dominion, she commandeered two extra loungers clearly marked “Reserved”. When two young travellers politely reclaimed them, she huffed, “We’re staying here! How dare they!” Dana gave Bryan a look that said we’re disowning our nationality, while Bryan wondered if she’d melt if she touched seawater.

A few metres away, a Russian man provided a different sort of entertainment, chain-smoking an entire sampler pack of cigarettes. His smoke drifted over in aromatic waves, ensuring that even the most devoted yoga-breather couldn’t hold a peaceful inhale for long. “Is this the price of luxury?” Bryan muttered, waving a menu like a surrender flag.

Still, paradise persisted. The staff were endlessly attentive, raking the beach as if each grain of sand were under inspection. Lunch arrived at their loungers: pizza crisped to perfection, a cold beer beading in the heat. Swim, sunbathe, read, repeat, the holy trinity of beach days.

As dusk approached, they swapped saltwater for skyline, heading to INK360, the rooftop bar atop the InterContinental. The lift opened onto the 19th floor and the world simply… fell away. The horizon stretched in all directions, sea bleeding into sky in a soft blur of indigo and gold. A warm wind curled around them, carrying the faint scent of lime and perfume. Below, the lights of Phu Quoc shimmered like dropped jewels, and somewhere a DJ eased jazz into chill-out beats. Dana stood silent, hair lifted by the breeze, eyes wide, the kind of silence that means awe, not boredom. Bryan, naturally, ordered cocktails,The Lantern and Hugo Sprits “for artistic appreciation.” For a few perfect minutes, neither spoke. The world was still.

On the drive home, city lights flickered past, and when they spotted a night market bursting with colour, they did what any self-respecting travellers would: they told the driver to stop immediately. Under strings of fairy lights they bartered, snacked and laughed, intoxicated by the warm evening air.  Between the lanterns and lychee stalls we found a man selling left-handed chopsticks, he swore they improved flavour by 12 percent, Dana bought two.

The night ended, as all good travel days should, in a massage parlour of questionable tranquillity. It was Dana’s first full-body massage, and she emerged radiant, floating, declaring it “life-changing.” Bryan’s experience was less zen. The neighbouring guests seemed to believe they were at karaoke night. After politely asking for quiet twice, the third outburst sent Bryan storming into the hallway, towel clutched dangerously low, to tell them in his finest British tone to “shut up, please!” The silence that followed was instant and absolute. Dana has never laughed harder.

Despite forecasts of rain, the sun had shone all day, proof that sometimes the weather listens to those who deserve it.

They returned to their room sandy, sun-kissed and a little smug, the day replaying in lazy smiles. Tomorrow would bring more chaos, but tonight they slept with the sound of distant waves and the faint echo of Bryan’s diplomatic victory ringing in the air.

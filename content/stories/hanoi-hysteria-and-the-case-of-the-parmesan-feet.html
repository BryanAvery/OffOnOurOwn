Singapore airport, 7 a.m. local time — or, as our jet-lagged bodies insisted, midnight. Changi was an airport that felt more like a five-star botanical museum: carpeted corridors muffling every footstep, koi fish gliding under glass floors, and orchids spilling over in perfectly photogenic chaos. Even the air smelt faintly of jasmine and luxury. Dana stood there wide-eyed, clutching her coffee like a pilgrim in a holy place. “This is an airport?” she said. “In Prague, we just get a vending machine and a man snoring.”

The transfer to our Hanoi flight took all of five minutes — one gate to another in record time — so we wandered just long enough for Bryan to contemplate buying a duty-free bottle of gin “for emergencies.” Then three hours later, somewhere over the South China Sea, both of us drifted off mid-sentence.

Arrival in Hanoi – The Warm Scent of Chaos

Stepping out of Hanoi airport was like opening an oven door scented with motor oil and incense. The air wrapped around us — thick, humid, full of life — and the smell was a heady mix of street food smoke, exhaust fumes, and adventure. Within seconds, a chorus of “Taxi? Taxi? Very good price!” surrounded us. Fortunately, our hotel driver stood calmly under pillar number ten, holding up a neat sign like an oasis of order in a traffic symphony.

Then the drive began. Imagine a video game where the goal is to not hit anything while simultaneously honking every four seconds. Our driver weaved through scooters, buses, and pedestrians who appeared entirely fearless. He was also texting. Dana turned to Bryan with that tight Czech smile that translates to “I regret my life choices.”

First Impressions of the Old Quarter

By the time we reached the Old Quarter, the world had become a blur of horns, scooters, and tangled power lines. The Oriental Signature Spa Hotel was a beacon of calm: a gleaming lobby, soft music, and a welcome drink so fruity and colourful it looked designed for Instagram. “We might never leave,” Dana sighed as the air conditioning kissed her skin.

But jet lag could wait — curiosity couldn’t. So, map in hand, we stepped out into the storm of Hanoi life.

The Temple, the Trypophobia, and the Dumpling Incident

The walk to Hoan Kiem Lake felt like being part of an endless parade: scooters gliding past with entire families balanced on them, street vendors pushing carts of steaming corn, and women in conical hats arranging marigolds in baskets. The lake shimmered through the smog like a mirror of calm. We crossed the scarlet bridge to Ngoc Son Temple, a tiny island sanctuary surrounded by turtles and bonsai trees.

It was beautiful — until Dana spotted a giant floral mural made of hundreds of tightly packed blooms. Her trypophobia flared like a fire alarm. “Why would anyone do that?” she whispered, stepping back as if it might lunge at her. “It’s like the flowers are watching me.”

Lunch followed at a tiny café with plastic stools the size of flowerpots. Spring rolls arrived first — crispy, safe, delicious. Then came the dumplings.

They glistened. Translucent, wobbling, filled with some mysterious grey-pink paste. Bryan leaned in with the camera. “Go on, give it a try,” he said, eyes twinkling.

Dana squinted. “They look like… little aliens.”

“They’re prawns,” Bryan assured her.

“They’re see-through. Prawns are not see-through.”

She poked one with her chopstick, watched it jiggle, sighed deeply, and, like a woman walking to her fate, took a bite. The texture — slippery yet chewy — caused her face to perform an entire one-act play. First suspicion, then confusion, then startled surprise.

“Hmm,” she said at last, chewing thoughtfully. “It’s… actually good.”

“Another one?”

“No.”

Bryan laughed so hard the locals stared.

The Great Foot Redemption

After several hours of weaving through the manic maze of Hanoi’s backstreets, our feet felt like they’d been replaced with bricks. So when we saw a glowing “Foot Spa – Relax Time” sign, we dived in.

We were led upstairs, handed hot lemon water, and told to soak. “Bliss,” Dana murmured — right until Bryan’s masseuse caught sight of his feet, gasped dramatically, and disappeared, returning with what looked suspiciously like a cheese grater.

It began. Scrape, scrape, scrape. White curls of Bryan drifted to the floor. Dana was howling with laughter. “They could use you to garnish pasta,” she said between giggles.

Then came the massage proper — firm, relentless, almost spiritual in its pain. Bryan drifted off, snoring gently, until the masseuse jabbed him awake with a knowing grin.

By the end, our feet felt like clouds — soft, weightless, redeemed. Bryan’s pride slightly less so.

Dinner and the Night Market Madness

Dinner at MIA was an aromatic feast. Platters of crispy pork, shrimp with star fruit, and fragrant rice filled the table. Bryan drank coconut water straight from the shell, while Dana sipped watermelon juice, looking radiant and revived. “It’s like home,” she said dreamily, “if home had 10,000 scooters and better food.”

Then came the night market — a living river of humanity. The streets pulsed with energy: sizzling woks, neon lights, vendors shouting prices above the roar of engines. It was impossible not to get swept up — literally, as the crowd moved like a slow, determined current.

The smell was intoxicating — grilled corn, fresh lime, incense, and something unidentifiable but oddly delicious. Dana was in sensory overload. Every five metres she’d stop at another handbag stall, examining gleaming “designer” bags. Louis Vuitton, Gucci, Chanel… or their distant cousins: Louie Vuttonn, Goochi, and Channelle.

After a few failed negotiations (and much dramatic sighing), she finally found the one: a sleek “Lulu Vutin” number for £19. “It’s perfect,” she beamed. “And genuine!”

“Genuine what?” Bryan asked.

“Genuine happiness,” she replied — which, to be fair, was true.

We ended the evening laughing under strings of paper lanterns, the sounds of karaoke drifting across the rooftops. Hanoi was mad, messy, marvellous — a city that didn’t sleep, didn’t slow down, and definitely didn’t care about traffic rules.

Tomorrow, we sail into serenity — or at least that’s the plan. But for tonight, we let the chaos carry us, one foot (and fake handbag) at a time.

If Hanoi is an orchestra of honking scooters, sizzling woks, and shouting vendors, then The Oriental Jade Hotel is that glorious moment when the conductor lowers the baton and everyone takes a breath. Tucked neatly in the Old Quarter, it became our little sanctuary — a cool retreat from the glorious madness outside, where the air smelled of lemongrass and success (ours, mostly, for surviving another Hanoi crossing).

Our Grand Arrival (and Immediate Melting)

Stepping into the lobby was like walking through a portal. Outside, a thousand motorbikes buzzed by carrying everything from families to furniture. Inside, polished marble, quiet smiles, and cold passion-fruit drinks.

Dana’s eyes lit up at the chandelier, while Bryan was already trying to spot the bar. The staff greeted us so warmly that we immediately forgave the fact we’d just sweated through our clothes.

Our room was simple, stylish, and cool — all jade tones and soft lighting. The bed was surprisingly hard, but in that firm, oddly perfect way that left us both sleeping like contented cats. From the window, St. Joseph’s Cathedral peeked out across the rooftops, while down below, scooters continued their symphony of chaos.

Rooftop Views & Hanoi Beer Nights

Up on the rooftop pool, life slowed to a lovely shimmer. The water glittered, the skyline glowed, and we both had cold glasses of Hanoi craft beer in hand. No cocktails this time — we went local, and it was a brilliant choice.

We watched the sunset paint the city gold, feeling very smug about how cultured we were, and congratulated ourselves on finding the perfect hotel. Bryan did not drop his sunglasses in the pool (a personal best), and Dana declared the whole experience “Instagram-worthy, but actually nice in real life too.”

Breakfast at the Jade

Breakfast here was a buffet of dreams — steaming phở, buttery pastries, tropical fruit, and coffee strong enough to power a small generator.

Bryan tucked into banana pancakes and croissants like he was being paid by the bite. Dana went straight for the local Vietnamese dishes, which she absolutely loved — noodles, soups, savoury pancakes, and rice that somehow tasted like sunshine.

We left breakfast each morning feeling heroic, slightly overfed, and buzzing from coffee that could strip paint.

Spa Temptations and Street Discoveries

Although the hotel had its own luxurious spa, we were seduced by one a few doors down. A friendly smile, the faint scent of eucalyptus, and the promise of a bargain was all it took. We both emerged ninety minutes later looking slightly dazed, smelling divine, and wondering if we could move in.

No hangover, no stress — just two very relaxed humans floating back through the Hanoi heat, giggling about how we’d spent less than a London sandwich on an hour of pure bliss.

Why We Loved the Oriental Jade

It wasn’t just the air-con or the jade-green tiles. It was how the place seemed to breathe calm into the chaos. After each adventure — whether negotiating cyclo rides, dodging scooters, or bartering in the markets — we’d step through those glass doors and feel instant peace.

The staff were unfailingly kind, the rooftop view was magical, and the location couldn’t be better — right in the middle of everything, yet just far enough to sleep without dreaming of horns and honks.

Final Thoughts from Bryan & Dana

Bryan loved the hard bed, Dana loved the breakfast. We both loved the rooftop, the beer, and the feeling that we’d stumbled upon one of Hanoi’s little treasures.

As we checked out, Dana sighed, “I don’t want to leave.” Bryan nodded, gazing fondly at the buffet, and said, “Maybe we should just fake our passports and stay.”

No hangovers, no regrets — just happy memories, cool sheets, and a newfound respect for firm mattresses.
